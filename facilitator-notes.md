# Facilitator Notes: GenAI Foundations Workshop

A slide-by-slide run sheet for delivering this session. Pairs with `GenAI-Foundations-Workshop.pptx` (the speaker notes on each slide are the short version of what's below).

## Before you run this

**Audience:** Non-technical staff attending Tier 1 (Foundations) — assume little to no prior AI tool use, and a wide range of comfort from curious to anxious.

**Room setup:** Slides visible to everyone, plus a way for participants to write during the practice section (their own laptop/phone, or the printed `hands-on-exercise.md` worksheet).

**What to prepare beforehand:**
- Swap the placeholder task example on Slide 3 (parent-communication email) for one genuinely relevant to your audience's actual daily work.
- Test whatever AI tool you'll demo live, right before the session — don't rely on it having worked yesterday.
- Pull your organization's real AI usage policy so Slide 9 points to something concrete, not a generic principle.

## Common facilitation pitfalls

- **Explaining all five framework steps before anyone tries one.** People retain the framework far better after seeing it used once than after hearing it defined abstractly. Build the live example on Slide 6 before moving to Slide 7's comparison.
- **Rushing the practice slide because it's "just an exercise."** It's the actual point of the session — everything before it is setup, everything after it is reinforcement. If you're short on time, cut content earlier, not practice time.
- **Answering "will this replace my job?" with reassurance instead of specifics.** A vague "don't worry" lands worse than naming one concrete task AI helps with and one it can't touch, for their actual role.
- **Letting the safety slide feel like a compliance lecture.** Frame it as protecting them individually (from an avoidable mistake), not just protecting the organization.

## After the session

- Send the recap slide (11) and the worksheet as a follow-up within 24 hours.
- Note who seemed stuck during practice — a two-minute individual follow-up prevents quiet non-adoption.
- Log rough attendance and any self-reported confidence shift; this feeds an adoption scorecard for measuring the program over time.
